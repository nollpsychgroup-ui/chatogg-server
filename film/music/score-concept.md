# THE QUIET CAR: Score Concept

**Composer** · based on `script/draft-02.md`, `notes/director-notes-01.md` and `camera/visual-approach.md` · prep document, expect changes once the script locks

---

## 1. The one idea: the score is the lights

In a film about a car that listens, a conventional score would be a lie: someone outside the carriage telling us how to feel. So **the score never shows up as "music."** It lives inside the carriage, as the hum of the fluorescent panels.

- The train runs on mains power. The ballast hum is **50 Hz** (the script reads as UK: Holloway, "Mum"). That's roughly **G1, 35 cents sharp**, and it is the film's tonal centre. The whole score is tuned to it (A4 ≈ 449 Hz). If we shoot on a 60 Hz rig, everything moves to ≈ B♭1/B1 and the system still works.
- **Each ceiling panel sings one partial of the harmonic series on 50 Hz.** The DP plans about 12 panels, so panels 1 to 11 carry partials 2 to 12 (100, 150, 200 … 600 Hz), with Noor's panel on partial 2. **The farthest panel is the deliberate impurity:** it skips ahead to **partial 17 (850 Hz, ≈ A♭ +5¢)**, the car's own note (see §2). The far end of the carriage already has a little of the car in it. It's the first panel to go dark, and the last to relight. Played together, a harmonic series fuses into one timbre, so a **fully lit carriage just sounds like hum**. The audience never hears a chord, only "the train." That's the point.
- **The car breathes.** From the first groan until the last relight, the hum swells **±3%, one cycle every 5 seconds**, locked to the DP's panel breathing. When the last panel relights, the breathing stops dead.
- **When the car is woken, the hum stops being innocent.** A lie bends a panel's partial out of tune. In the early lies the bend is small (≈10–20¢), felt as wrongness rather than heard as notes. **The first time the hum is clearly out of tune is Kai's brownout** (≈70–90¢ flat), so the escalation in sound matches M2. When a panel goes dark, its partial disappears. When it relights, the partial comes back.

**For the first few minutes there is no score at all, by design (Director's brief).** The carriage chord is perfectly fused and in tune: the audience hears an ordinary fluorescent hum, and so does the mix. Music begins at Noor's first lie, when the hum first starts, very slightly, to come apart. **The music is the car slowly waking.**

So the score tracks the DP's lighting plan one-to-one. **One panel equals one voice.** The darkness creeping toward Noor is literally the chord being taken apart, high partials first, so the car sounds duller and heavier as it approaches. Her confession rebuilds it one true sentence at a time.

## 2. The car's motif: THE UNDERTONE

The car's voice sits **below** the hum, an octave under the fundamental (G0 ≈ 25 Hz). On a big system it is felt as much as heard. **On a laptop or phone the 25 Hz vanishes**, so the motif has to live in its doubling at 100–200 Hz plus the bowed-metal grain (energy up to ~2 kHz). That is the layer that must carry the motif on its own (see §8, phone check).

**Motif:** a slow half-step orbit around the hum pitch: **G → A♭ → F♯ → G**. It's the shape of a breath: up on the inhale, sag past centre on the exhale, then settle. It never resolves anywhere new. It always comes back to the hum, because the car is patient.

It escalates with each waking (Director's note **M2**: the rule has to be visible and audible):

| Waking | Lights (DP cue) | Undertone |
|---|---|---|
| Noor boards, straightens lanyard (**M3**) | 0, tick | one partial wobbles for 2 frames, no Undertone |
| Lie 1: "Just leaving the office now" | 1, one panel | single voice, quiet; partial bends ≈10¢ |
| Lie 2: "The Henley account" | 2, two panels | two voices (G and A♭ rubbing), louder and longer; two partials bend ≈20¢ |
| Kai: "I'm at the library, Mum" | 3, brownout | full three-voice cluster (F♯ G A♭), and every partial sags flat by ~70–90 cents: **the first audible out-of-tune moment**, the whole carriage going sour |
| "My name is S—" (**M1**, Walter grabs her wrist) | 5, hard dip | the "hungry" swell (draft 02): the cluster rises out of the floor and settles only as Walter's grip holds. The audience learns the car hears *intent* |
| "I'm f—" | 6, stutter | one partial bends, then snaps back on "I'm not fine" |
| Panels going dark toward Noor | 7, sequential out | cluster rises slowly under the thinning chord |

**The inhale in the blackout is sound design**, not score. My only contribution is a pitch reference: if the wet breath rises F♯ → G (the motif reversed), it will be in tune with the hum we've been hearing for three minutes, and it will feel like it came from the walls. No music plays in the blackout.

## 3. Where music stays out

Silence is the premise, so the score's biggest job is **leaving**. Agreed with the Director's brief and the DP: **score is out wherever the picture is perfectly still.**

- **Opening to the first lie:** no score. The hum is just hum.
- **The blackout is true dead air** (DP cue 4, shot 5 s+, about 3 s on screen). Digital zero: no hum, no rails, no room tone, no score tail. Everything cuts on the same frame as the lights.
- **Truth does nothing, and so does the score.** On every true line ("My name is Noor," Bea's "I haven't slept in two days," Walter's wife), the hum stays steady and in tune. There are no warm strings for the sad story. The music's indifference to the truth *is* the car's rule.
- **The C3 breath (8 → 5 min):** hum and rails only.
- **Noor's thumb over Kai's call (draft 02):** **rails alone.** That's the Director's ruling, and the hum sits only at room-tone level.
- **The "library" half-beat in the confession:** the warmth layer (§6) holds still. Nothing new enters on it.
- **The PA line itself:** no score. The hum carries it.

## 4. Palette and instrumentation

The rule is that **every sound should be deniable as the train**, so we can't tell where design ends and music begins.

- **Carriage chord:** sine and near-sine tones with a little ballast buzz (2nd/3rd harmonic), recorded through a small speaker in a real carriage or IR'd to one. Optional doubling with **bowed vibraphone and glass** at very low level for the upper partials.
- **The Undertone:** sub-sine plus **contrabass** (sul ponticello, very slow bow, scordatura down to G0), **bowed cymbal / bowed bass drum** for metal resonance, and resampled train sounds (brake squeal, coupling groan) pitch-locked to G/A♭/F♯.
- **The confession's warmth:** **two cellos in natural harmonics** plus a little glass, each doubling a partial as its panel relights. This is the only vibrato in the film, so it's the only sound that is plainly human.
- **The release:** bowed vibraphone and glass, softer than the cellos.
- **Door chime and PA chime:** tuned to the series (partials 18 and 12: A and D above the hum). It's a small thing, but it makes the train's own interface part of the harmony.
- **Not used:** piano, a full string section, synth pads with obvious attacks, risers, braams, percussion hits on scares, and melody of any kind.

## 5. Score vs. sound design: who owns what

| Element | Owner | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| THE GROAN | **Sound design** | Music gives SD the pitch map (G / A♭ / F♯ around the hum) so the groan and the Undertone never fight |
| THE UNDERTONE | **Music** | Tuned bed under the groan. Separate stem, also feeds the DP's seat transducers if sound agrees |
| Panel hum (carriage chord) | **Built by music, mixed as sound design** | 12 stems, one per panel and DMX channel, plus the 50 Hz fundamental, so panels can be muted and detuned exactly to picture. Until the first lie it is simply room tone |
| Inhale in the blackout | **Sound design** | Music supplies a pitch reference only |
| Relay ticks and clicks (cues 0, 7, 8) | **Sound design** | Dry, on the lighting frame. Music doesn't "hit" ticks; a partial simply stops or starts under them |
| Rails, brakes, doors, room tone | **Sound design** | Rails go to digital zero in the blackout |
| Phone murmur, Mum's V.O., PA voice | **Dialogue/SD** | |
| Door chime | **SD, tuned by music** | I'll supply the pitches |
| Confession warmth (relight cue) and release | **Music** | The only two score cues in the conventional sense, and both nearly subliminal |

**Mix principle:** the hum is never louder than room tone would plausibly be. If the audience notices "music" anywhere other than the confession, we've played it too loud.

## 6. The confession and the ending

**The relight cue (the film's one moment of warmth).** The panels relight **from Noor outward** (Director's ruling): the dark came toward her and the truth spreads out from her, so the far A♭ panel comes back last. As each panel relights (DP cue 8), a cello harmonic quietly doubles that panel's partial with a slow, narrow vibrato. The warmth arrives with the light, not under the words. The lines themselves still get nothing, and the relights are not still picture.

- The cue builds an open sonority: **G, D, F and A** (partials 4, 6, 7 and 9). The 7th partial F is the "blue" note, slightly flat.
- **The third is withheld.** B (partial 5) is never doubled, so the chord is neither major nor minor and doesn't resolve. Mark's answer is withheld, and so is the music's.
- When the line goes silent ("I'm sorry." / silence on the line), the cellos hold and the vibrato freezes into straight tone.
- **The cue thins out as the train slows** and is gone before the doors open.

**"Thank you for riding quietly."** The door chime and the PA line get no score. Per the DP and Director there's no parting light cue either: the car stays dead stable, and the completed hum, in tune and no longer breathing, carries Noor's exit and the PA.

**The release, on Walter's page turn.** It's a pure 4:5:6 triad on **partials 8, 10 and 12** (G, B, D in just intonation, three to three and a half octaves above the hum), on bowed vibraphone and glass. **The B is the third the confession withheld.** It arrives for Walter, the first human gesture in the film that isn't defensive, not as a verdict on Noor's marriage.

- **Nearly subliminal**: softer than you'd think, slow attack, no vibrato. These are pitches the hum has held all film, so it should feel like the hum briefly lit up from inside, not like "music arriving."
- **It's gone before Kai's phone goes dark.**
- **The hum stays under the final shot until the cut**, with the fundamental and lower partials intact, as scripted ("The lights hum."). The car is still there.
- **Cut to black:** rails only, as written, then stop. **The title card and credits are silent** (Director's ruling).

## 7. Light/sound sync points (DP cues 0–8, `camera/visual-approach.md`)

All of these need a lighting cue and a sound change on the **same frame**. The cue sheet will reference the DP's cue numbers 1:1.

| DP cue | Picture | Hum / score |
|---|---|---|
| 0 tick | panel above Noor −15% for 2 frames | partial 2 wobbles, almost nothing |
| 1 flicker | one panel | first music: one partial bends, Undertone ×1 |
| 2 flicker | two panels | two partials bend, Undertone ×2 |
| 3 brownout | every panel warm and dim | whole chord sags ~70–90 cents flat, Undertone cluster |
| 4 blackout | true black | **everything to zero on the frame** |
| — lights up | panels back | hum back instantly, in tune and indifferent |
| 5 hard dip | Noor's panel to ~5%, neighbours dip | Undertone "hungry" swell |
| 6 stutter | two hiccups | one partial bends and snaps back |
| 7 sequential out | one panel at a time, far end first | one partial removed per tick, the A♭ panel first, then highest to lowest; Undertone rises |
| 8 relight | one panel per true sentence, **outward from Noor**, 110% overshoot | partial returns with a ~+1 dB swell; cello harmonic doubles partials 4/6/7/9 |
| — page turn | no light change | release triad, nearly subliminal, gone before the phone dims |

- **Master clock:** agreed. On set, the console/QLab fires each light cue and its sound partner by timecode.
- **Breathing:** agreed. From the first groan to the last relight, the hum swells ±3% at one cycle every 5 s, locked to the panels. Then dead stable.
- **Relight order:** outward from Noor (Director's ruling).
- **Still to confirm with the DP:** exact panel count (assumed 12). The partial assignment depends on it.
