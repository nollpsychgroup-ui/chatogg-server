# THE QUIET CAR: Visual Approach

**DP prep document** · Based on `script/draft-02.md` (Director-approved, awaiting the human gate) · **Approved by the Director** (see `notes/dp-notes-01.md`); their decisions are folded in.

---

## The idea in one line

**The ceiling panels are the monster.** The car has no face, so the lights stand in for one. They are how it looks at you, breathes, and reaches for you. The camera holds still and lets the lights perform.

Four rules come out of that:

1. **A dead-stable baseline.** While the car is asleep the light never changes. There is no "atmospheric" flicker, no passing-light wobble across faces, no stray movement. Every change in the light must mean something, so we can't spend any on mood. If the audience catches one flicker that doesn't follow a lie, the rule stops working.
2. **The truth does nothing.** When someone tells the truth, the frame stays flat, even and unchanged. The calm is the reward, and it should feel almost unbearable after a cue.
3. **The panel row is a meter.** The audience should be able to *count* the panels. The opening wide is shot down the length of the car and shows the whole row lit. The same master frame comes back at every escalation (1 panel, 2 panels, the whole car, the dark creeping up the row, the relight), so the viewer reads how bad things are from how many panels are lit. It's a gauge, not a backdrop.
4. **The camera doesn't flinch.** When the car reacts, the camera holds. We don't whip-pan to a flickering panel or crash-zoom on a scream. A locked frame reads as evidence: *this really happened, and nothing was cut.*

## Format

- **Aspect ratio: 2.39:1.** A carriage is a long, low tube, and the ceiling panels run its length like a fuse. The wide frame puts the whole row of panels in shot, so the creeping darkness in act three crosses the frame from one side to the other and we can watch it come. It also traps people: there's ceiling above them, floor below, and nowhere to go at the sides.
- **Spherical lenses, not anamorphic.** Anamorphic flares and edge breathing would add "accidental" light events, which break rule 1. We crop to 2.39 from an open-gate or 16:9 sensor, which also gives us room to reframe.
- **Camera:** a digital cinema camera with dual native ISO and a clean deep-shadow floor (ARRI Alexa 35, Sony Venice 2, or equivalent). The film lives at the bottom of the exposure range, during the brownout, the creeping dark and the phone-lit faces, and those shadows have to hold detail without noise until the moment we choose to go truly black.
- **Frame rate and shutter:** 24 fps at 180°. Every LED fixture must use **high-frequency PWM drivers (≥ 20 kHz) or constant-current dimming**, or dimmed panels will strobe and roll on camera. That has to be confirmed in a camera test before the shoot, and nobody hires a fixture without it.
- **Real time.** The LED destination display counts down 11 → 0 minutes, and the cut should honour it as closely as possible. The display is a practical prop that we program and is legible in frame, so it acts as the film's clock. No title cards.

## Lensing

A small set of matched primes: **25, 32, 40, 50 and 75 mm** (Super 35 equivalents). The lens grammar follows Noor's honesty.

| Story stage | Lens | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Noor performing "fine" (opening to Kai's vanishing) | **25–32 mm**, from a distance | She is small among the seats and the panels. We watch her from across the car the way Walter does: observed, not inside her head. The wide lens shows the whole room so the audience learns where everything is: doors, sign, display, the four seats. |
| Truth exchange (8 → 5 min) | **40–50 mm** | It's conversational and human. People finally talk to each other, and the lens moves closer. |
| The creeping dark (5 → 2 min) | **32 mm** for the row of panels, **50 mm** for Noor | Wide shots track how far the dark has come. Close shots show her face starting to understand. |
| Confession | **75 mm**, the only time we use it | It's the closest we ever get to her. It is saved for the moment she stops performing, so the first long lens in the film arrives with the first full truth. |
| Ending | Back to **32 mm** | The doors close and we step away again, inside with Walter and Bea, watching her go. |

**Camera height:** just below seated eye-line, tilted very slightly up, so **the ceiling stays in frame whenever the car is awake.** While the car sleeps, singles can frame the ceiling out. Once it wakes, the panels creep back into the top of frame and stay there. Most viewers won't notice consciously that the ceiling is watching.

**Focus:** a moderately deep stop (T2.8–T4). Keeping the panels and the far end of the car sharp matters more than creamy backgrounds, because the background is where the threat is.

## Camera movement

- **Default: locked off** on sticks or a static dolly. The train moves and the camera doesn't. **The camera moves only while the car is awake.** Every move listed below happens between the first groan and the last relight, and while the car is asleep the frame never moves.
- **Slow dolly pushes**, motivated only by the car advancing: one creep toward Noor during the sequential panel-outs, matching the dark's pace, and one push toward her through the confession on the 75, which **lands and stops by the final relight (cue 8, last panel)**, so the frame is already still when the car goes back to sleep. They should be slow enough that the audience feels them before they see them.
- **One handheld passage:** Noor at the doors yanking the emergency handle after Kai vanishes. It's the only time the frame loses control, because it's the only time Noor does. As soon as Walter holds up the newspaper, we go back to sticks.
- **No camera shake for the groan.** The set itself vibrates (see *Selling the carriage*), and the camera sits on that floor, so any tremor in the frame is physical, not added in post.

## Palette

| Element | Colour | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Asleep (baseline) | **Cool fluorescent white, ~4300 K with a touch of green** | Unglamorous transit light, flat and even, with slight under-eye shadows. It should feel safe and dead. |
| Awake / brownout | **Sodium orange, ~2200 K, about 2.5 stops down** | Real fluorescents don't go orange, but audiences read a warm, dim brownout as a power sag, a failing system, something drawing current. We dim the tunable panels *warm* as they go down. |
| Blackout | **True black** | 0 IRE, no fill, and no passing light in the windows. The LED display goes dark too. |
| Windows | Black, with occasional cold tunnel lights passing | Passing lights appear only while the car is asleep, which keeps rule 1, and **only in the glass**: the window rig is flagged so it lights the window frames and reflections, never faces or panels. During every cue the windows go dead black: the car closes its eyes to the outside. |
| Phone screens | Cold blue-white | They are the only light the car doesn't control. In the final dark stretch, Noor's **MARK** screen lights her face from below. |
| Platform (ending) | Single **cold-white practical**: a mercury-vapour or blue-white LED street lamp, not moonlight | **Director's decision.** Orange has to mean only *the car is awake*. The platform is plain and unflattering: the uncoloured light of the truth she now has to stand in. Mark's withheld answer carries the ambiguity. The relit carriage and the platform are two temperatures of "nothing hidden." Keep the light ground haze. |

**Production design asks (for the Director to pass on):** seats in a muted teal moquette, grey laminate walls, and no reds anywhere in the car. Red is kept for **Noor's lanyard strap**, plus one other red (Director's addition): **the red end-call button on Kai's phone**, which must stay readable in the 40 mm insert. It ties her badge to the call she can't end. Phone UIs carry no other red. The strap is the only saturated object in the car, so the M3 plant (the tick above her as she straightens it) and the moment she throws it on the floor are both easy to follow. Her suit is charcoal, Bea wears navy scrubs, Walter a camel overcoat, and Kai a grey hoodie. Everyone sinks into the car except the badge.

## How the lighting carries the horror

### The rig

- **About 12 overhead panels** (to match the set's length), each an **individually addressable, tunable-white (ideally RGBW) LED unit** behind the set's own diffusers, on its own DMX channel and running at high PWM frequency.
- **Panel numbering (agreed with the Composer):** 12 panels, provisional until the set is measured. **P1 is the panel above Noor, and P12 is the far end.** The dark creeps from P12 toward Noor (cue 7) and stops with only P1–P2 lit. The confession relights run back the other way, nearest the dark edge first (P3 → P12), so **the far end relights last**. Each panel is one DMX channel and one partial in the Composer's hum, with stems mapping 1:1.
- **A lighting console with cue stacks** (or QLab driving DMX), **linked by timecode to sound playback**, so each light cue fires together with its groan on set. The actors react to something real, and the editor gets cues that were already in sync on the day.
- **Separate channels** for: the destination display, the door-chime light, the window passing-light rig, and the platform sodium lamp.
- **No film lights inside the car.** Everything we see is lit by the ceiling panels, the phones and the windows. Any extra fill is bounced from just outside frame and **ganged to the same DMX cue as the panel it supports**, so it dims and dies with that panel. If a cue kills the room, there's nothing left lighting the actors.

### The vocabulary: one cue per kind of lie

Each cue has a fixed look and a fixed sound partner (see *Sync*). The escalation follows Director's note M2: **the more the car is woken, the bigger its answer.**

| # | Cue | When | Look |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | **The tick** (M3 plant) | Noor straightens her lanyard on boarding | One panel above her drops ~15% for **2 frames** and returns. It's subliminal on first viewing and plain once you know. Only Walter reacts. |
| 1 | **Flicker, one panel** | Lie 1, "Just leaving the office" | The panel above her, three or four irregular pulses down to ~30% over about half a second. |
| 2 | **Flicker, two panels** | Lie 2, "The Henley account" | Two panels, longer and deeper (to ~10%). The car is listening more closely. |
| 3 | **Brownout** | Kai: "I'm at the library, Mum" | **Every** panel sags warm and dim to sodium orange and *holds*. It's the first time the whole car acts at once. |
| 4 | **Blackout** | Kai's second lie | A clean snap to true black: panels, windows, display, everything. About **3 seconds** on screen, long enough to become unbearable. **Shot at 5 s or more** so the edit has room. |
| 5 | **Hard dip** (M1) | "My name is S—", cut off by Walter's hand | The panel above Noor slams to ~5% and the two beside it dip in sympathy, then they recover slowly once she stops. |
| 6 | **Stutter** | The reflex "I'm f—" | One panel, two quick hiccups. It's smaller, because the lie never finished. |
| 7 | **Sequential out** | The 5 → 2 minute stretch | One panel at a time, from the far end toward Noor, **snapping straight off** with a relay *tick*. No flicker: this isn't a reaction any more, it's an advance. The darkness is something approaching, not a malfunction. |
| 8 | **Relight** | Each true sentence in the confession | One panel at a time, *click*, snapping on with a brief **overshoot to ~110%** that settles to baseline within half a second (the script's "burning brighter"). The last panel returns the room exactly to the opening state. |

### The car waking

Lights alone could read as faulty wiring. What sells the car as *alive* is what happens to the light **between** cues:

- **It breathes.** From the first groan until Noor's confession, every lit panel carries a very slow swell of **±3% at about one cycle every 5 seconds**, in time with a low breath in the sound bed. **At the test it's checked on the monitor and projected large; if anyone can see it, drop to ±2% or slow it down.** It must be felt, not seen. It's below conscious notice, but the audience will feel the room breathing. When the last panel relights after the confession, **the breathing stops**: the light goes back to dead stable, and the car is asleep again. **There is no parting cue.** After the last relight the car stays dead stable to the end; the only movement left in the frame is Walter turning the page and Kai's phone screen dimming. The car looks completely innocent.
- **It shuts out the world.** While it's awake there are no tunnel lights in the windows. The car is sealed in its own attention.
- **It moves.** Each groan, and the Composer's Undertone stem, is played through bass transducers bolted under the seats and floor, so the set physically hums. Coffee rings in a cup, the newspaper trembles, and the actors feel it in their seats. The camera picks up a faint real tremor.

That's the "waking" arc: **dead stable → breathing → acting → advancing → retreating → dead stable.** The audience should be able to feel where the car is on that line from the light alone, with the sound off.

### Kai's vanishing, done in camera

My recommendation is **a single locked-off take with no cut.** Kai is lit at the brownout, then cue 4 takes the frame to true black. In the dark, Kai slips out of his seat and through a slot in the wild wall, guided by IR (infrared) safety light. **The actual camera, filter and ND stack is tested to confirm it records nothing from the IR sources**, since some combinations pick up near-IR, and one glowing frame would ruin the vanishing. The phone is already on the seat. After the blackout (shot at 5 s or more) the lights snap back up on the same frame. The seat is empty.

A cut hidden in the black frames would also work. But a locked frame that never moved is more frightening, and if we hold the wide we can show the whole car, so the audience can see he hasn't simply moved to another seat.

### The creeping dark

- Shot on the **32 mm from Noor's end looking down the car**, so the dark comes *toward camera*, one panel at a time.
- As each panel goes out, its section of the car goes properly black, not just dim. We need flags or snoots on the diffusers so there is no spill between sections and the dark has a **hard edge** travelling up the aisle.
- Kai's phone, abandoned in the dark section, keeps its small blue glow: the boy left in the dark that the car has already taken. (Its glow is set at 8 min, when Noor crosses to end the call and can't. We hold a 40 mm two-shot of her thumb over the red button, lit only by the screen and the steady overhead light.)
- By "two panels left," Noor is lit half from above and half by her phone's **MARK** glow from below. When she answers, the phone side of her face is the stronger light.

## Selling one practical carriage, with no VFX

1. **A built set or a borrowed retired carriage, with wild walls.** A borrowed carriage gives us authenticity; a build gives us removable walls and somewhere to hide the rig. If we borrow one, we light it entirely through its own fittings, swapping the fluorescent tubes for our addressable LED units in the same housings.
2. **Motion without moving.** Movement comes from three things together:
   - **Passing lights**: an LED strip chase or swinging lamps on a frame outside the windows, used only while the car is asleep.
   - **Rocking**: if the set is built, it sits on air bags or a small rocker run by grips; if we borrow a carriage, a gentle physical nudge on the body. It's barely noticeable, and it stops dead whenever the car acts.
   - **Sound**: rails and the bass transducers. The rails' rhythm is sound's job, but the floor should shake with them.
3. **Black windows are an advantage.** A late-night train in a tunnel or over unlit country has black windows, so we don't need any exterior plate work. The black also gives us **reflections**: the panels and passengers mirrored in the glass. That's a cheap second image of the panel row, and a free scare when a panel goes out and its reflection goes with it.
4. **The platform at the end** is a short flat, a few metres deep, built against the open doors. It has one sodium lamp and a small amount of ground haze, with darkness behind (duvetyne, black fabric). We never see more of it than fits through the doors.
5. **The train pulling away.** We move the outside, not the train: Noor and a short section of platform floor sit on a rolling wagon that's pulled slowly back as the sodium lamp slides along with it, while the window chase starts up again and the set rocks. Through the closed door glass, she slides out of frame. All of it is practical.
6. **Everything on screen is practical.** The phones are real devices running scripted screens (MARK, MUM, the dimming at the end). The destination display is programmable. The newspaper margins are handwritten props, one per beat, so the actor's handwriting isn't left to chance.

## Sync with sound and music

This needs to be agreed with the Composer; I'm messaging them separately. My proposal:

- **The console is the master clock on set.** Light cues and their sound partners fire together from one cue stack. Sound designers and the Composer then refine timing in post, but we start from sync captured on the day.
- **Fixed pairings** (sound partner in brackets): tick (relay click, almost inaudible) · flicker (groan, faint) · two-panel flicker (groan, longer and closer) · brownout (groan from the *walls*) · **blackout (total silence, the rails cut too)** · the inhale in the dark · lights up (rails resume) · hard dip (groan swells and recedes) · stutter (no groan, or barely one) · sequential out (relay tick per panel, groan rising underneath) · relight (relay click per panel, groan receding) · final hum · cut to black (rails, then nothing).
- **The breathing light and any breathing in the sound bed share one tempo.**
- **Silence is a lighting event too.** Wherever the picture goes perfectly still (truth moments, the blackout), I'd like the score out as well, so the stillness lands at full weight.

## Technical requirements to flag for the Producer

- Twelve or so **DMX-addressable, tunable-white LED panels** with high-frequency PWM, plus a console or QLab, plus a **programmer/board operator** on set for every shooting day.
- **Bass transducers** for the seats and floor, with playback linked to the console.
- **A blackout-tight set:** every exit sign, monitor glow and gap taped or flagged. True black is the film's biggest scare, and it can be spoiled by one stray LED.
- **IR safety lighting** so actors can move in the blackout, plus a test that the camera records none of it.
- **A camera and lighting test day** before the shoot: PWM flicker check, the deep-shadow floor, the brownout colour, a check that the blackout really reaches black, the breathing pulse viewed projected large, and an IR leakage check.
