## Notes

- **Fix a pitch claim in §1.** Partial 13 is not near A♭: it's about **E♭ +41 cents** (≈841¢ above G). The harmonic nearest A♭ is **partial 17** (≈105¢). The idea that "the far end of the carriage already has the car in it" is lovely, so keep the idea and change the means: either (a) tune the far panel's partial to the car's A♭ *by design*, a deliberate impurity at the end of the row, or (b) just let partial 13's natural sourness be "the car's colour" without naming A♭. I lean towards (a), but it's your call. Also check the "octave and a half above the hum" wording for the release triad: partials 8, 10 and 12 sit about three octaves up.
- **Small speakers.** Plenty of people will watch this online on a laptop or phone. The Undertone at 25 Hz will vanish there, so make sure the 50/100 Hz doubling and the bass drum/cymbal grain carry the motif on its own. Do a phone-speaker check of the sketch's lie-2 and brownout sections.
- **Partial detuning must stay deniable until Kai.** In the early lies, the split in the hum should be *barely* audible, felt as wrongness rather than heard as notes. The first time the audience clearly hears the hum go out of tune should be Kai's brownout. That keeps the escalation from M2 working in sound as well as light.
- **The release triad: keep it nearly subliminal.** It's Walter's release, not a verdict on Noor's marriage. If it's even slightly too loud or warm, it will answer the question Mark's silence leaves open. It should be softer than you think, and it must be gone before Kai's phone goes dark.
- **The end hum.** My brief said "the fluorescent hum carries it to black." Your plan thins the hum from the top as the triad blooms, which is fine, but please leave **some** hum (at least the fundamental) under the final shot until the cut, as scripted ("The lights hum. CUT TO BLACK."). The car is still there. The rails then carry over black, as written.
