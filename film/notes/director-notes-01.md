# Director's Notes: THE QUIET CAR, Draft 01

**Verdict: Not approved yet, but close.** This is a strong, shootable draft. The structure is right, every scare is practical, it stays in one carriage with four actors, and the confession climax with the panels relighting is the image the film will be remembered for. The notes below are about **rule consistency** and **stakes**. The audience has to trust the rule absolutely or the ending won't land. Please address the **MUST** notes in draft-02. The **CONSIDER** notes are your call.

---

## What's working (keep it)

- **Opening.** Noor's two lies get a flicker and a groan while she stays oblivious, and Walter and Bea react. Establishing the rule from the regulars' faces before Noor understands it is exactly right.
- **Kai's vanishing.** The brownout, the blackout with the rails also going silent, the wet inhale, then the empty seat and "Kai? ... Kai?" from the phone. It's cheap and terrifying, the best sequence in the draft.
- **"I'm f—" / "I'm not fine."** It's a perfect small beat. It teaches the audience that the rule reaches reflexes, and it sets up the climax.
- **The library rhyme.** Kai's lie ("I'm at the library") is Noor's actual daily truth. Keep it. Consider letting Noor clock it (see C2).
- **The panels going dark toward her, then relighting one per true sentence.** The film's visual spine.
- **The ending.** We stay inside, Mark's reply is withheld, and Walter finally turns the page. Don't touch it.

---

## MUST fix

**M1. The "My name is Sarah" test undercuts the stakes.** Noor has just watched a boy vanish for lying. A deliberate lie, a flicker, and nothing more tells the audience the car is lenient, and that deflates everything after it. Keep the test, but make it cost something. Suggested version: "My name is Noor." Nothing. "My name is S—", and Walter's hand clamps her wrist *mid-word*. The panel above her dips hard and the groan swells, then settles as she stops. The rule is demonstrated, the danger stays total, and Walter gets a physical beat.

**M2. Rule consistency: why Noor survives two lies and Kai doesn't.** Both tell two lies. Right now the only answer is "the more it is woken, the more it wants," which lives in the format note, not on screen. Put it on screen. Make the escalation unmistakable: lie 1 gives one panel and a faint groan, lie 2 gives two panels and a louder groan, and Kai's lie 1 brings the brownout from the *walls*. Then have Bea or Walter make it explicit afterwards in a few words, written in the newspaper margin under DON'T LIE IN HERE. Something like: **IT'S AWAKE NOW. YOU WOKE IT.** That one line also loads Noor with guilt for Kai, which pays off in her confession.

**M3. Seed "it isn't only what you say" in the first two minutes.** The lanyard and costume turn is the smartest idea in the script, but at the moment the rule expands at the 5-minute mark with no warning, and it can feel like the film changing its rules. Plant it early and silently: when Noor boards and straightens her lanyard, the panel directly above her gives a single, almost subliminal *tick*. No groan, and nobody reacts except Walter, whose eyes go to the badge. Viewers who catch it will feel smart later, and those who don't will still feel the turn is fair.

**M4. Fix Walter's line count.** The direction before "He's asking." says it's "the only thing he has said aloud to her all night that isn't about himself," but he already said "It isn't only what you say." Either cut that direction or reassign "It isn't only what you say" to Bea. I prefer giving it to Bea: she's the nurse, she recognises a body that's performing well, and it gives her a bigger role in the third act.

---

## CONSIDER

**C1. Noor's guilt about Kai.** Right now Kai is forgotten once he's gone. One beat in the confession would tie the threads together. The emotional climax is about Mark, but a glance at Kai's glowing phone before she answers ("He's asking") would let us feel what Noor's lie set in motion.

**C2. The library line.** Noor's confession includes "I go to the library and I sit there." Let her hear it as she says it: a half-beat in which she looks at Kai's phone. It's a small, bleak echo that doesn't need a word.

**C3. The truth exchange is a little brisk.** Bea's "I haven't slept in two days" and Walter's wife story work, but the 8-to-5-minute stretch is the film's breath before the third act. Give it one more silent beat of rails and stillness, maybe Noor reaching for Kai's phone to end the call, then not doing it. In real-time horror, the quiet passages are what make the ticks frightening.

**C4. Mum's V.O.** That means a fifth voice in post. It's cheap, but flag it for the Producer. It should be as degraded as Mark's murmur, intelligible only on "Kai?"

---

## Technical notes for later (no script change needed)

- Every lighting cue (flicker, stutter, brownout to orange, blackout, sequential panel-out, sequential relight) means the ceiling panels must be **individually addressable and dimmable**: LED practicals on a DMX board. This is the single biggest technical requirement, and I'll put it in the treatment.
- "The rails go quiet too" during the blackout is a great sound note. Silence is our biggest scare, and the mix must allow true dead air.
- Kai's disappearance is a hard cut in the blackout. No continuity trick is needed.
