# Director's Notes: camera/visual-approach.md (v1)

**Verdict: APPROVED as the visual approach.** This is exactly the film. "The ceiling panels are the monster," the dead-stable baseline, and the camera that doesn't flinch are now the visual rules of the production. The 9-step cue vocabulary, the 75 mm saved for the confession, handheld only at the emergency handle, and Kai's vanishing in one locked take are all approved as written.

## Your two decisions

**1. Platform light: cold white, not sodium.** I see the appeal of the echo, but I'm going the other way, for two reasons:
- **The ambiguity is already carried.** Mark's withheld answer is the film's open question. Putting her in the car's waking colour adds a second ambiguity on top, and the two compete.
- **Rule clarity.** We've spent 8 minutes teaching the audience that orange means *the car is awake.* If the last exterior is orange, some viewers will read it as "the car followed her," which is a different ending.

So the platform should be **cold, flat and unflattering: a mercury-vapour or blue-white LED street lamp, not moonlight.** It isn't hopeful. It's just *plain*, the uncoloured light of the truth she now has to stand in. Keep the single practical and the ground haze. The relit carriage behind her and the plain platform in front of her should read as the same kind of honest light, two different temperatures of "nothing hidden."

**2. No reds except Noor's lanyard strap: approved.** I'm passing it to the Producer for production design. Extend it to the props as well: no red on the phones' UI except the **red end-call button** on Kai's phone, which is the one other red object. That links her lanyard and the call she can't end. Please keep that button readable in the 40 mm insert.

## Notes

- **Breathing pulse (±3% / 5 s): approved, with one condition.** It mustn't read as a flicker on screen, or it breaks your own rule 1. At the camera test, check it on the monitor *and* projected large. If anyone can spot it consciously, drop to ±2% or slow it down. It should be felt, not seen.
- **Passing tunnel lights while asleep:** keep them in the **glass only**. No spill should travel across faces or panels, or we'll get the stray "light events" you've banned. Flag the window rig so it lights the window frames and reflections, not the people.
- **IR safety light in the blackout:** add a test that the actual camera and filter stack records *nothing* from the IR sources. Some sensor and ND combinations pick up near-IR. One glowing frame in the blackout would ruin the vanishing.
- **Blackout length:** about 3 seconds on set is right. Edit may shorten it, but shoot it long (5 or more seconds) so we have the room.
- **Ending, after the relight:** I want the car back to dead stable with *no* final cue. Don't add a parting flicker. The only movement left in the frame is Walter turning the page and Kai's phone screen dimming. The horror of the ending is that the car looks completely innocent.
- **Reflections in the black windows:** a great find. The cue 7 shot where a panel goes out and its reflection goes with it can be in the shot list.
