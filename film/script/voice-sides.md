# THE QUIET CAR: Voice Sides

**Source:** `script/draft-02.md` (LOCKED). Every spoken line below is verbatim from the locked script. Nothing is added to the on-screen dialogue. The only extra material is **unintelligible phone beds** (Mark, Mum) and **non-verbal efforts** (breaths, gasps, crying). These are performance material and don't change the script. They're marked ⚑ for the Director to approve.

**Why this matters:** under `FORMAT.md` the voice track is recorded first and the animation is built to it. Every pause, breath and cut-off here becomes a timing the animators will match frame by frame, so the take notes are deliberately precise.

---

## How to read these sides

**Line IDs** run `CHARACTER-##` in story order. **Cue** refers to the DP's lighting cue numbers in `camera/visual-approach.md` (0 to 8). Those cues are the shared sync language for CG, music and edit.

**Level scale.** Four levels. Hold them consistently, because the mix depends on them.

| Level | What it means | Rough guide |
|---|---|---|
| **WHISPER** | Unvoiced or barely voiced, breath-led. Noor's quiet-car phone voice. | Mouth 15–20 cm from the mic. Don't project. |
| **LOW** | Voiced but hushed. Normal speech for people in a quiet car who know what lives in it. | Like speaking to someone asleep in the next room. |
| **FULL** | Normal conversational volume. *It reads as loud in this car.* Only Kai, and Noor at her panic peak. | Don't push to shouting unless noted. |
| **PHONE** | Recorded clean at the level noted, then degraded in post (band-limited, low level, under the rails). | The actor performs at normal phone level, *close*, as if into a handset. |

**Mic and room:** a dead, dry booth for all carriage voices. The carriage acoustic comes from the real-carriage impulse response in post (`FORMAT.md`), so *don't* perform "roominess". Phone voices are recorded through a real handset into a second mic as a safety, as well as clean.

**Cut-offs.** Several lines are cut off mid-word, and each cut-off is different: *interrupted by another person*, *interrupted by the car*, *self-caught*, or *trailing away*. The type is noted on every cut-off line. Always record **a long version that runs past the cut**, so the edit can choose the exact frame, plus the clean cut performed.

## Recommended session order

1. **Ensemble session: Noor, Kai, Bea, Walter**, in separate isolated booths with sightlines and talkback, recorded **in story order**. The film runs on near real time and on listening. Overlaps ("I can't —" / "*Say it.*"), Walter's wrist clamp and Kai's laugh into the blackout all need live partners. Play the **groan and cue stack as temp audio in cans** at the right moments, so actors react to the car the way the audience will.
2. **Noor solo pickups:** the confession again, alone, as many passes as she needs. It's the climax, and she's on screen for every minute of it.
3. **Phone voices:** Mark and Mum, each performed *live against Noor's and Kai's recorded lines* in their cans, so the beds breathe with the scene.
4. **Automated Voice:** one line plus alts.
5. **Non-verbals and wild track** for all four carriage voices (see the end of this document).

## Character notes for performance

- **NOOR (40s).** Her "fine" is excellent: warm, competent, practised. The performance is *the slow failure of the performance*. Early on she's pleasant and slightly harried, managing everyone, even the stranger on the phone. Lower and quieter is not the same as weaker. The confession is **crying with a level voice**: the face breaks while the words stay steady, because she has finally stopped managing anything.
- **KAI (19).** Instantly likeable. Cocky, tired, a bit of a show-off, obviously somebody's son. His death must feel unjust, so play charm, never edge. He's the only one who speaks at FULL, and not out of rudeness: he simply doesn't know.
- **BEA (50s).** A night-shift nurse. Her default is the patient voice: soft, unhurried, warm, *practical*. She has used it on frightened people a thousand times. Under it she's exhausted, two days without sleep. When she breaks out of it ("*Say it.*"), it should shock.
- **WALTER (70s).** He has almost no lines, so every one is chosen. He's economical, unsentimental, has nothing to prove, and is long past pretending. His wife speech is said *plainly*: he has said it to himself so often there's no performance left in it. That is what makes it devastating.
- **MARK (phone).** We never make out a word, but we must **hear love in the murmur**. He's patient, a bit worried, a man who has been gently not-asking for weeks.
- **MUM (phone).** Warm, a bit exasperated, very ordinary. She has no idea she's in a horror film, and that's the point.
- **AUTOMATED VOICE.** Pleasant, neutral, prerecorded, faintly corporate. It must sound like a real network PA, *not* sinister. The irony does the work.

## THE SIDES

### Scene 1: Boarding and Mark's first call (11 MIN)

**NOOR-00 ⚑ (non-verbal)**
*Context:* she steps in out of breath and drops into a seat. Cue 0 (the tick) lands as she straightens her lanyard.
*State:* end of a long day, relieved to sit down.
*Level:* breath only.
*Take notes:* a slightly winded exhale as she sits, then a smaller "settling" breath as she smooths her skirt. **Don't** react to anything. Nothing has happened as far as Noor knows. Give 3 takes, of different lengths.

**NOOR-01**
> Hi. Hi, yeah. I'm on the train.

*Context:* she answers Mark with a glance at the QUIET CAR sign and an apologetic wince at no one.
*State:* warm, tired, a little guilty about breaking the quiet-car rule (the only rule she thinks exists).
*Level:* WHISPER.
*Take notes:* the first "Hi" is caught as she lifts the phone. The second "Hi, yeah" is properly to him, with a smile in it. "I'm on the train" is **true**: easy, unguarded, no weight. It's the baseline we'll measure her lies against.

**MARK-01 ⚑ (phone bed, unintelligible)**
*Context:* under NOOR-01 and NOOR-02. Is she on her way? How was her day?
*State:* fond, a little careful.
*Level:* PHONE.
*Suggested text to perform (never heard intelligibly):* "Hey, you. You on your way? ... How was it today?"
*Take notes:* the text is only scaffolding for real intonation. The audience hears the *shape* of a question and the warmth. Leave natural gaps where Noor answers.

**NOOR-02** · *Cue 1 follows (one-panel flicker)*
> Just leaving the office now.

*Context:* **Lie 1.**
*State:* automatic. She has said this sentence every evening for weeks. It's fluent, practised and pleasant. **There's no tell.** This is the most important note in Noor's first scene: if she sounds like she's lying, the audience gets ahead of the film.
*Level:* WHISPER.
*Take notes:* say it exactly as easily as "I'm on the train." Leave about a second of silence after it before her next line, because the flicker and the faint groan play in that gap.

**NOOR-03** · *Cue 2 follows (two-panel flicker)*
> Long day. The Henley account. Again.

*Context:* **Lie 2**, an elaboration. She's padding the story with detail, as practised liars do.
*State:* a little performed weariness and a fond-exasperated eye-roll in the voice. She's *enjoying* the normalcy for a second.
*Level:* WHISPER.
*Take notes:* three clean phrases with small gaps. "Again" lands as a little shared joke between them, an account Mark has evidently heard about before. Leave about 1.5 seconds after it for cue 2 and the longer groan.

**KAI-01**
> Was that the brakes?

*Context:* he pulls out an earbud and looks around after the second groan.
*State:* mildly curious, not scared. He's half-amused.
*Level:* LOW. It's to nobody, but he isn't whispering. He's the kind of person who wouldn't.
*Take notes:* a throwaway. The audience should almost miss it. Give a slight upward lilt, a "huh" quality.

**NOOR-04**
> No, don't wait up. Love you.

*Context:* she wraps up the call, oblivious.
*State:* tender, genuine. "Love you" is **true**, and it should sound it.
*Level:* WHISPER.
*Take notes:* "No, don't wait up" is a kind dismissal. Then a half-beat and "Love you", softer and real. It's the only fully honest thing she gives Mark until the confession, so make it count without leaning on it.

**NOOR-05 ⚑ (non-verbal)**
*Context:* she hangs up, sags, looks up, and finds Walter and Bea staring. She mouths "Sorry". **The word is mouthed, not voiced.**
*Level:* breath only.
*Take notes:* a sag-exhale as the phone drops. Then a tiny intake as she notices them watching, and a breathy, voiceless shape for "Sorry", which the animators will use to lip-sync. Make it *sheepish*.

### Scene 2: Kai's call (still 11 MIN; cues 3 and 4)

**KAI-02**
> Yeah?

*Context:* his mum calls. He rolls his eyes and answers.
*State:* teenage-son weariness, affectionate underneath.
*Level:* **FULL.** It's the first full-volume voice in the film, and it should feel loud in the quiet car.
*Take notes:* one syllable with the whole relationship in it: "*what now*, Mum".

**MUM-01 ⚑ (phone bed, under KAI-02 and KAI-03)**
*Context:* where is he, it's late.
*State:* warm, mildly exasperated, ordinary.
*Level:* PHONE.
*Suggested text (unintelligible in mix):* "Where are you? It's gone eleven, Kai."
*Take notes:* the audience hears only the music of a mum checking in.

**KAI-03** · *Cue 3 follows (brownout)*
> I'm at the library, Mum.

*Context:* **his lie**, said over the rumble of a train. It's obviously, comically false to us.
*State:* easy, cocky, a little smug. He's told this one before, and it always works.
*Level:* FULL.
*Take notes:* a breezy delivery with a grin in it. "Mum" lands slightly patronising, the way nineteen-year-olds talk to their mothers. **No hesitation.** He doesn't know there's a rule. Hold about 2 seconds after it for the brownout and the groan from the walls.

**KAI-04** · *Cut-off type: INTERRUPTED BY THE CAR (cue 4, blackout)*
> No, that's — it's the library. They're doing work on the —

*Context:* the lights are orange and the walls are groaning. Mum has evidently heard something.
*State:* the cockiness cracking. A **nervous laugh** leads into the line. He's starting to be scared but is still trying to style it out, because he can't explain it to Mum.
*Level:* FULL, dropping slightly through the line as nerves win.
*Take notes:*
- The first "—" is a self-correction mid-thought. Restart quickly.
- **The final cut-off is done in the edit**, a hard cut to digital silence on the blackout frame (score note: true dead air). **Don't** perform the cut. Keep going right through it: "They're doing work on the — on the lines, or, I dunno, it's—" and run on for 2 or 3 seconds. The edit cuts mid-word, ideally on an open vowel, so the silence feels like a mouth being closed.
- Record 3 or more takes with different levels of fear, from still mostly joking to really rattled. The Director picks.

**MUM-02** · *After LIGHTS UP*
> Kai? ... Kai?

*Context:* Kai's seat is empty. His phone is face-up with the call still connected.
*State:* at first just puzzled: has the line dropped? On the second "Kai?" a thread of worry comes in. **Not** panic. She doesn't know anything is wrong yet. That ordinariness is the horror.
*Level:* PHONE, recorded clean and close, then degraded tiny and tinny in post.
*Take notes:* the only intelligible phone line in the film (Director's note C4). Leave a natural pause of 1.5 to 2 seconds between the two. Record extra loose "Kai?"s and "Hello?"s for the later recurrences while the phone sits on the seat (the "*Kai?*" beats at 8 MIN and on). Each should be quieter and more resigned, as if she's about to give up.

### Scene 3: After the vanishing (cue 5)

**NOOR-06** · *Cut-off type: SELF-INTERRUPTED (panic outruns language)*
> What — where did he —

*Context:* she's on her feet staring at the empty seat.
*State:* raw shock, the first time the "fine" breaks.
*Level:* LOW, rising. It's involuntary, not addressed to anyone.
*Take notes:* two failed sentences. The first "—" is a breath catch. The second trails into her stumbling toward the doors, so **record it with the movement**: have her actually get up and take a step in the booth if she can, or simulate the exertion. Give a long version where it runs on into breath and movement noise.

**NOOR-07 ⚑ (efforts)**
*Context:* she pulls the emergency handle, which won't move, and pulls again.
*Level:* efforts.
*Take notes:* two pulls. The first is an effort grunt, the second harder and more desperate.

**NOOR-08**
> Hey. *Hey!*

*Context:* to the door, the driver, anyone.
*State:* fear tipping into anger.
*Level:* first "Hey" LOW to FULL, second **FULL**, her loudest in the film.
*Take notes:* the first is a question and the second a demand. This is the moment she stops caring about the quiet-car sign, so let it ring.

**NOOR-09**
> That's insane.

*Context:* she reads the margin notes: DON'T LIE IN HERE / IT'S AWAKE NOW. YOU WOKE IT.
*State:* disbelief that is already losing to belief.
*Level:* **barely audible.** It's the bottom of WHISPER.
*Take notes:* she isn't telling Walter it's insane. She's trying it out on herself, and it doesn't hold. It's **true** to her as she says it, which is why nothing happens. Flat, small, almost no pitch.

**NOOR-10**
> My name is Noor.

*Context:* she tests the rule.
*State:* frightened, careful, scientific. She's speaking *to the ceiling*.
*Level:* LOW.
*Take notes:* slow and deliberate, each word placed like a foot on ice. Then **hold**, because the silence afterwards is the test result. Leave 2 or more seconds after the line in every take.

**NOOR-11** · *Cue 5 (hard dip) · Cut-off type: INTERRUPTED BY ANOTHER PERSON (Walter's hand)*
> My name is S—

*Context:* the second half of the test, a lie. Walter clamps her wrist **before the word finishes**.
*State:* the same careful voice as NOOR-10, with a hair more defiance: she's going to prove it.
*Level:* LOW.
*Take notes:*
- The cut lands **on the /s/**: about 100 to 200 ms of sibilant hiss, **no vowel**. "Sss—", not "Sa—". The car only hears the start of the lie, and the audience must too.
- What stops her is **physical**: a hand grabbing her. The /s/ snaps off into a **sharp involuntary intake**, a startled gasp, and then *nothing*. Nobody breathes.
- Run it live with Walter, so he grabs her actual wrist in the booth if both actors agree, or squeezes a shared hand-hold across the glass on a visual cue. The animators need the true startle.
- After the gasp, hold the breath for about 3 to 4 seconds while the panel dips and the groan swells. Then give a shaky, controlled release as the groan settles. Then a *shaking* breath or two as Walter lets go.
- Record clean-cut takes and long takes, where "My name is Sar—" overruns, so the edit can choose the frame.

**WALTER-00 ⚑ (non-verbal)**
*Context:* the wrist clamp.
*Take notes:* a single short exhale through the nose as he grabs, with effort and urgency in it. **No** vocal sound beyond that. Then he holds his breath with everyone else.

### Scene 4: The quiet beat and the truth exchange (8 MIN; cue 6)

**NOOR-12 ⚑ (non-verbal)**
*Context:* she crosses to Kai's phone and reaches to end the call. Her thumb hovers. She can't.
*Take notes:* one held breath through the hover and a small, defeated release as she withdraws. **No** sobbing. It's pure restraint, and nearly inaudible.

**BEA-01**
> You okay?

*Context:* the first thing Bea says out loud.
*State:* the patient voice, soft and unhurried, a professional kindness, beneath it her own exhaustion.
*Level:* LOW, very soft.
*Take notes:* she's asking a real question, not making small talk. Pitch it like a night nurse at 3 a.m. at the end of a bed.

**NOOR-13** · *Cue 6 (stutter) · Cut-off type: SELF-CAUGHT*
> I'm f—

*Context:* a **reflex** lie. The social auto-reply.
*State:* automatic, then frightened of herself.
*Level:* LOW.
*Take notes:*
- The cut lands **on the /f/**: a short fricative, *no vowel*. "Ff—", not "Fi—".
- Unlike NOOR-11, **nobody stops her**. She hears herself, and the stop is a **soft swallow-catch**: lips closing, a stopped breath. It's quieter and more inward than the gasp at the wrist clamp. These two cut-offs must sound different, because the audience learns the rule partly from the difference.
- Then: "She stops. Breathes." Give **one** slow, careful breath of about 2 seconds while the panel stutters and steadies.

**NOOR-14**
> I'm not fine.

*Context:* the correction, and the first *chosen* truth she tells.
*State:* careful, almost relieved. It's the first time she has said it out loud to anyone.
*Level:* LOW.
*Take notes:* plainly. No crying yet. There's a tiny surprise in it, at how easy that was and how much it costs. Play it to Bea.

**BEA-02**
> Me neither. I haven't slept in two days.

*Context:* the nod first, "as if that's the right answer to everything."
*State:* warm solidarity. She's offering a truth back, like a trade.
*Level:* LOW.
*Take notes:* "Me neither" comes quickly, almost a laugh, rueful. "I haven't slept in two days" is matter-of-fact, the way nurses talk about their own bodies. **True**, and easy.

**NOOR-15**
> You've seen this before.

*Context:* to Walter.
*State:* this is a realisation, not a question. A little accusing.
*Level:* LOW.
*Take notes:* a statement, with a downward inflection.

**WALTER-01**
> I ride it every night.

*State:* simple fact. No drama, no menace.
*Level:* LOW.
*Take notes:* he's not being mysterious. Give it the flatness of "I take the 8:15."

**NOOR-16**
> *Why?*

*State:* incredulous, almost angry. Why would anyone *choose* this?
*Level:* LOW, with a spike of energy. She keeps it down because she's learned to.
*Take notes:* one word with the whole night in it.

**WALTER-02**
> My wife was ill a long time. Everyone told me she'd be fine. Doctors. Her sister. Me, to her.
> (beat)
> This is the only place nobody can say that to me.

*Context:* he folds the paper along its crease and takes his time.
*State:* plain. **Unsentimental.** He has said this to himself so many times there is no performance left in it.
*Level:* LOW, steady.
*Take notes:*
- **Leave a pause before he starts.** The fold of the newspaper is his breath. Give it 2 or 3 seconds of paper-handling noise in the take if you can (wild track too).
- "Doctors. Her sister." is a list, said evenly. "**Me, to her.**" is the hardest line he has. It's barely slower and barely quieter. **Don't** crack the voice. Let the listener crack.
- *(beat)*: 2 to 3 seconds.
- "This is the only place nobody can say that to me" is almost gentle, with something like gratitude in it.
- Record a version where he's slightly drier, and one where he's slightly warmer, for the Director to choose.

### Scene 5: The dark comes (5 → 2 MIN; cue 7)

**NOOR-17**
> Nobody said anything.

*Context:* the far panel has gone out with a tick, with no flicker.
*State:* fear plus confusion. *That's not the rule.* It's a protest.
*Level:* WHISPER to LOW.
*Take notes:* partly to them and partly to the car, a little fast.

**BEA-03**
> It isn't only what you say.

*Context:* she's reading Noor's body like a patient who insists they're fine: the blazer, the badge, the empty laptop bag.
*State:* a clinician's recognition, gentle but **certain**. She has seen this before in patients.
*Level:* LOW, quiet.
*Take notes:* the key is the stress on **only**. Don't make it an accusation. It's a diagnosis given kindly.

**NOOR-18 ⚑ (efforts)**
*Context:* she yanks the lanyard over her head and throws it on the floor.
*Take notes:* a sharp effortful breath. Something like anger at herself. Then her breathing quickens through the next tick.

**BEA-04**
> Whatever it is, say it.

*State:* urgent, still in control.
*Level:* WHISPER, with *urgency*: an intense, voiced whisper.
*Take notes:* fast and even. She's coaching a patient through something.

**NOOR-19** · *Cut-off type: INTERRUPTED BY ANOTHER PERSON (Bea)*
> I can't —

*State:* terrified and ashamed, close to tears for the first time.
*Level:* WHISPER.
*Take notes:* she's about to explain ("I can't — he'll —"). Bea overlaps on the dash. **Record live in the ensemble** so the overlap is real, and run past it so the edit can choose.

**BEA-05**
> *Say it.*

*Context:* it lands on top of "I can't —".
*State:* Bea finally breaks out of the patient voice. It isn't a shout. It's **command**, the way you'd talk to someone who has stopped breathing.
*Level:* LOW, with full force behind it. It should shock because it's the first time Bea has pushed.
*Take notes:* two words, both stressed. It must cut Noor off. Give 3 to 4 takes with different overlap timing.

**WALTER-03**
> He's asking.

*Context:* Mark's call is buzzing in her lap. She has just looked at Kai's phone, whose call is still open: "someone else is still asking."
*State:* *very gentle*. He isn't pushing her. He's simply naming what's in front of her. It's the kindest thing in the film.
*Level:* LOW, the quietest of his lines.
*Take notes:* the pause before is long, about 2 seconds. Give a slight emphasis on **asking**. The line is about Mark and also, by the look Noor has just given, about Mum. Walter knows that too.

### Scene 6: The confession (2 MIN → arrival; cue 8)

> **Performance note for the whole scene:** *crying, with a level voice.* Her face breaks (the animators will do that), but **the words stay steady**, because for the first time she isn't managing anything. Each true sentence brings a panel back on, *click*, so **leave a clean gap of 0.5 to 1 second after each sentence** for the relight. **Don't** let the crying mask consonants. Every word must be intelligible: the audience has to hear each truth land. Record the scene in one pass, over and over. Don't build it from pieces.

**NOOR-20**
> Mark.

*State:* she has decided. It's a held breath released into his name.
*Level:* LOW, *not* whispered. She's no longer hiding from the car or from him.
*Take notes:* a single word, steady, "I'm here, and I'm going to tell you."

**MARK-02 ⚑ (phone bed)**
*Suggested text (unintelligible):* "Hey. You're late. Everything all right?"
*Take notes:* light, normal. He doesn't know what's coming.

**NOOR-21** · *Cue 8 begins: the panel above her steadies and burns brighter*
> No. Listen. I'm not leaving the office.

*State:* she's stopping him, then making a correction that is also an admission.
*Level:* LOW.
*Take notes:* "No." gently cuts him off. "Listen." is a breath of courage. "I'm not leaving the office" retracts lie 1, word for word in mirror. Play the mirror: same rhythm as "Just leaving the office now," but true.

**NOOR-22** · *Cue 8: first relight down the car*
> I haven't been to the office since September. They let me go. The whole floor.

*State:* the facts at last. The first tears are in the throat, the voice level.
*Level:* LOW.
*Take notes:* three sentences with three relight gaps. "They let me go" is the hardest. "The whole floor" is a small, pathetic defence: *it wasn't just me.* Let a little of the old management come through on that one.

**NOOR-23** · *Mid-line catch: self-caught, she hears herself*
> I get dressed and I get on the train and I go to the library —
> — and I sit there. All day. And then I come home.

*State:* the routine spilling out in one breath, until "library".
*Level:* LOW.
*Take notes:*
- The run-on "and ... and ..." comes in one breath, gathering speed. It's the shape of weeks of days.
- On **"library"**, she *hears it*. It's Kai's lie, now her truth. A **half-beat catch** follows, not a full stop: the breath suspends and her eyes go to his phone. About 0.5 to 0.8 seconds.
- "— and I sit there" restarts softer. "All day." Then "And then I come home." is the smallest line in the speech, said almost with shame at how small her life has got.

**NOOR-24** · *Cut-off type: TRAILING (she can't finish)*
> I didn't tell you because you'd have been kind about it, and I couldn't —

*State:* "crying now, but steady." This is the real reason, and the most exposing thing she says.
*Level:* LOW.
*Take notes:*
- "because you'd have been kind about it" is the heart of the film. She means it as an explanation, and it's also a love letter.
- "and I couldn't —" trails away. She *can't* say the end of the sentence (couldn't bear it, couldn't be the person who needed it). Let it **dissolve into breath**, not stop. Several panels click on in quick succession here (*click, click*), so leave about 2 seconds of crying breath after it.

**MARK-03 ⚑ (phone: silence)**
*Context:* "The murmur on the phone has gone silent."
*Take notes:* record 20 seconds of Mark **on the open line, listening**: handset room tone, the faintest breath, perhaps one swallowed intake. No words. It must feel like a person holding a phone, not a dead line.

**NOOR-25**
> I'm sorry.

*State:* small. Everything is said, and this is all that's left.
*Level:* **WHISPER**, the bottom of the scale.
*Take notes:* the voice is at its quietest and most level. Tears are audible in the breath, not in the words. Then hold. The last panel clicks on into "silence on the line."

### Scene 7: Arrival and departure

**MARK-04 ⚑ (phone bed, as she stands on the platform)**
*Context:* "Faintly from the phone, the murmur starts up again. We can't hear what he says."
*State:* **the audience must not be able to tell** whether it's forgiveness or anger. The Director's ending withholds his reply. Perform it *warm*: the Director's casting note asks for love in the murmur. The degradation makes it ambiguous, and Noor's stillness makes it unbearable.
*Suggested text (never intelligible):* "Noor. ... Okay. ... Okay. Come home. We'll— just come home."
*Take notes:* record warm, neutral and strained versions, so the Director can find the most ambiguous one in the mix.

**AUTOMATED VOICE-01**
> Thank you for riding quietly.

*Context:* the doors close between us and Noor. Prerecorded PA.
*State:* pleasant, bland, corporate. *Completely innocent.* (Directing note: the car must look completely innocent in the last shot. The voice is part of that.)
*Level:* FULL, network-announcer style. It's processed in post through PA speaker IR.
*Take notes:*
- **No irony in the read.** If the actor sounds in on the joke, it dies. Read it the way it would be read for the thousandth announcement in a real recording booth.
- Give a slight downward lilt on "quietly".
- Alts: (a) brighter, more customer-service; (b) flatter, more machine. Also give a "Now arriving: Holloway" alt and a "Next stop: Holloway" alt, for display/PA consistency if the Director wants them later. They're **not** in the locked script, so they're unused unless the Director and Producer approve.

## Non-verbal and wild track (all four carriage voices)

Record after the ensemble session, in character, with the cue stack in cans:

| ID | Voice | What | Notes |
