# THE QUIET CAR

**Draft 02** · Screenwriter (revised per director-notes-01) · Target runtime 8–9 min

> **Format notes:** One location, the last carriage of a late-night commuter train. Four on-screen actors. Mark, Noor's husband, is heard only as a faint, unintelligible murmur from her phone, so we never need a fifth performer. Every "scare" is a **practical lighting cue** (dips, cut-outs, ceiling panels going dark in sequence) or a **sound cue** (THE GROAN: a low, wet, metallic exhale, as if the carriage is breathing). Disappearances happen in a blackout and are revealed when the lights return. There are no visual effects.
>
> **The rule, as the audience learns it:** a lie flickers the lights and makes the car groan. Each lie wakes it further: one panel, then two, then the whole car. Once it is awake, the next lie is taken. Truth does nothing. It reads more than words: a lie you are *wearing* counts too.
>
> **Voices in post:** Mark (Noor's husband) and Kai's Mum are both phone-only, degraded and mostly unintelligible. Mum is intelligible only on "Kai?". Two small voice-over sessions, no on-screen actors.

---

**CAST**

- **NOOR** (40s): pressed office clothes, company lanyard, the posture of someone performing "fine."
- **KAI** (19): student, hoodie, earbuds, cocky and tired.
- **BEA** (50s): nurse in navy scrubs and a cardigan, eyes closed but not asleep.
- **WALTER** (70s): overcoat, folded newspaper. He has been on this car before.

---

### INT. LAST CARRIAGE — NIGHT

A late-night commuter carriage, nearly empty. Fluorescent ceiling panels run its length in a tidy row. On the end wall, a vinyl sign: **QUIET CAR**. Below it, an LED display reads: **NEXT STOP: HOLLOWAY**.

The low rumble of the train idling at a platform.

WALTER sits midway with his newspaper folded to one page. He isn't reading it.

Across from him, BEA has her head back and her eyes shut. Her hands are folded over her bag, very still.

Near the doors, KAI slouches with one earbud in, scrolling, his thumb glowing.

DOOR CHIME. NOOR steps in, a little out of breath, laptop bag on her shoulder, lanyard on. She takes a seat facing the others, straightens her lanyard so the badge sits face-out, smooths her skirt, and exhales like someone ending a long day.

As the badge settles, the panel directly above her gives a single, almost subliminal **tick**. There's no flicker to speak of and no groan. Nobody reacts except Walter, whose eyes go to the badge, then back to his paper.

The doors close. The train pulls away. The LED display updates:

**NEXT STOP: HOLLOWAY — 11 MIN**

Silence, except for the rails.

Noor's phone BUZZES. The screen reads **MARK**. She glances at the QUIET CAR sign, winces apologetically at no one, and answers low.

**NOOR**
(whispering)
Hi. Hi, yeah. I'm on the train.

The murmur of a man's voice, too faint to make out.

**NOOR**
Just leaving the office now.

Overhead, **one** ceiling panel, the one above her, **flickers**. Once.

Beneath the rails, so faint it could be the brakes: **THE GROAN**. A long metallic exhale.

Walter's eyes lift from his paper and go to Noor. Bea's eyes open.

Noor doesn't notice.

**NOOR**
(into phone)
Long day. The Henley account. Again.

**Two** panels flicker this time, hers and the next one along, and hold their dip a beat longer. **THE GROAN** is louder, longer and closer, unmistakably not the brakes.

Walter lowers his newspaper completely. Bea sits up.

Kai pulls out his earbud and looks around.

**KAI**
(to nobody)
Was that the brakes?

**NOOR**
(into phone, oblivious)
No, don't wait up. Love you.

She hangs up and drops the phone in her lap. She sags. When she looks up, Walter and Bea are both staring at her.

**NOOR**
(mouthing)
Sorry.

She points at the QUIET CAR sign and makes a "my bad" face. Walter doesn't smile. Neither does Bea.

Noor looks away, unsettled.

---

Kai's phone BUZZES. The screen reads **MUM**. He rolls his eyes and answers at full volume.

**KAI**
Yeah?

Bea's hand shoots out, *don't*, but she's too far away.

**KAI**
I'm at the library, Mum.

**ALL THE LIGHTS DIP.** It isn't one panel or two. Every panel in the car browns out at once to a dull orange and holds there.

**THE GROAN**, loud now. It is coming from the *walls*.

Kai freezes. He looks at the ceiling.

**KAI**
(into phone, a nervous laugh)
No, that's — it's the library. They're doing work on the —

**BLACKOUT.**

Total darkness and total silence. The rails go quiet too.

Then, in the dark, a single wet *inhale*, close and huge, as if the carriage has drawn breath.

**LIGHTS UP.**

Kai's seat is empty.

His phone lies face-up on the cushion with the call still connected. From it, tiny and tinny:

**MUM (V.O.)**
(from phone, faint)
Kai? ... Kai?

The rails start up again, as if nothing happened.

---

Noor is on her feet.

**NOOR**
What — where did he —

She stumbles to the doors, grabs the emergency handle and pulls. It doesn't budge. She pulls again.

**NOOR**
Hey. *Hey!*

Walter quickly sets his newspaper on his knee, uncaps a pen and writes in the margin. He holds it up so she can see:

**DON'T LIE IN HERE.**

Noor stares at it. Then at him. Then at the ceiling.

Bea leans across, takes the pen from Walter, and writes underneath. Walter turns the paper back to Noor:

**DON'T LIE IN HERE.**
**IT'S AWAKE NOW. YOU WOKE IT.**

Noor reads it twice. Her eyes go to Kai's empty seat.

**NOOR**
(barely audible)
That's insane.

Nothing happens. The lights stay steady. Walter just waits.

Noor looks at Kai's phone, still murmuring "*Kai?*", and at the empty seat.

She swallows. She tests it, almost to herself.

**NOOR**
My name is Noor.

Nothing. The lights hold and the rails rattle.

**NOOR**
(beat)
My name is S—

Walter's hand **clamps her wrist** before she finishes the word. It's fast, and much stronger than he looks.

But the first sound is already out: the panel above her **dips hard**, almost to black, and **THE GROAN** swells up out of the floor, *hungry*.

Nobody breathes.

Walter doesn't let go. Slowly the groan settles back into the rails and the panel climbs back up to white.

He releases her wrist. She's shaking.

The LED display: **NEXT STOP: HOLLOWAY — 8 MIN**

---

Noor sits back down slowly, opposite the two of them, with her hands pressed between her knees.

A long beat of rails. Nothing but rails.

On the seat by the doors, Kai's phone is still lit, still connected. Faintly: "*Kai?*"

Noor gets up, crosses to it, and reaches out to end the call.

Her thumb hovers over the red button. She can't do it. She leaves the line open and goes back to her seat.

Another beat of rails. Bea watches her the whole time.

Bea leans forward and speaks very softly, the way you talk to a patient.

**BEA**
You okay?

**NOOR**
(reflex)
I'm f—

A panel **stutters** over her head.

She stops. Breathes.

**NOOR**
I'm not fine.

The panel steadies. Bea nods, as if that's the right answer to everything.

**BEA**
Me neither. I haven't slept in two days.

Nothing happens. It's true.

Noor looks at Walter.

**NOOR**
You've seen this before.

**WALTER**
I ride it every night.

**NOOR**
(incredulous)
*Why?*

Walter folds the newspaper along its crease and takes his time.

**WALTER**
My wife was ill a long time. Everyone told me she'd be fine. Doctors. Her sister. Me, to her.
(beat)
This is the only place nobody can say that to me.

The lights are perfectly steady.

Bea looks at him with real tenderness. She knew this already. They are regulars.

Noor glances down at her own lanyard, the plastic badge with her photo and a company logo, and turns it over in her fingers.

---

The LED display: **NEXT STOP: HOLLOWAY — 5 MIN**

A soft *tick* from the far end of the car.

The **farthest ceiling panel goes dark**. It doesn't flicker. It simply goes out.

Bea sees it first and stiffens.

**NOOR**
Nobody said anything.

*Tick.* The next panel goes out, one row closer.

Walter's eyes go from the dark panels to Noor, then to her lanyard, as they did when she boarded.

Bea looks at Noor the way she'd look at a patient insisting they're fine. She takes in the pressed blazer, the badge, the laptop bag that clearly has nothing in it.

**BEA**
(quietly)
It isn't only what you say.

*Tick.* Another panel. The darkness is creeping up the carriage toward her seat. **THE GROAN** rises underneath it, slow and patient.

Noor looks down at herself: the office clothes, the badge. She is dressed for a job she doesn't have.

She yanks the lanyard over her head and throws it on the floor.

*Tick.* Another panel goes out. It isn't enough.

**BEA**
(urgent whisper)
Whatever it is, say it.

**NOOR**
I can't —

**BEA**
*Say it.*

*Tick.* Only two lit panels remain between the dark and Noor. Kai's phone sits in the shadow, its screen glowing.

The LED display: **NEXT STOP: HOLLOWAY — 2 MIN**

Noor's own phone BUZZES in her lap. The screen reads **MARK**.

She stares at it. Then she looks across the car at Kai's phone, still glowing in the dark, the call with his mother still open. Someone else is still asking.

Walter, very gently:

**WALTER**
He's asking.

---

Noor answers.

**NOOR**
(into phone)
Mark.

The murmur on the other end.

**NOOR**
No. Listen. I'm not leaving the office.

The panel above her **steadies**, burning brighter.

**NOOR**
I haven't been to the office since September. They let me go. The whole floor.

*Click.* Down the carriage, a dark panel **comes back on**.

**NOOR**
I get dressed and I get on the train and I go to the library —

She hears it. A half-beat. Her eyes go to Kai's phone in the dark.

**NOOR (CONT'D)**
— and I sit there. All day. And then I come home.

*Click.* Another panel relights.

**NOOR**
(crying now, but steady)
I didn't tell you because you'd have been kind about it, and I couldn't —

*Click. Click.* Panel after panel comes on. **THE GROAN** recedes into the rails.

The murmur on the phone has gone silent.

**NOOR**
(small)
I'm sorry.

Silence on the line.

*Click.* The last panel comes on. The carriage is fully, flatly, fluorescently lit, the way it was at the start.

The train begins to slow.

The LED display: **NOW ARRIVING: HOLLOWAY**

Noor keeps the phone pressed to her ear. Whatever Mark is going to say, he hasn't said it yet.

---

The train stops. DOOR CHIME. The doors slide open onto a cold, empty platform.

Noor stands. She looks back at Walter and Bea. Walter gives her the smallest nod. Bea has closed her eyes again.

Noor steps off the train.

**We stay inside the carriage.** Through the open doors we watch her stand on the platform under a single sodium light, phone to her ear, listening.

Faintly from the phone, the murmur starts up again. We can't hear what he says.

DOOR CHIME. The doors close between us and her.

Over the speakers comes a pleasant, prerecorded voice.

**AUTOMATED VOICE (V.O.)**
Thank you for riding quietly.

The train pulls away. Noor slides out of frame, still listening.

Inside, Walter unfolds his newspaper and, for the first time, **turns the page**.

On the empty seat by the doors, Kai's phone screen dims and goes dark.

The lights hum.

**CUT TO BLACK.**

The rails continue for a moment over black, then stop.

**END.**
